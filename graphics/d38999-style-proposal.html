Where the colors come from. MIL-DTL-38999 and the manufacturer catalogs define each finish by plating material + coating standard + a verbal color — there is no RGB/hex or FED-STD-595 number in the spec or any catalog in this repo. The hexes above are representative renderings of those real finishes. Verbatim sources in docs/pdfs/: Glenair Series I-IV p.C-1 (codes NF / ZN / MT), Amphenol & ITT Cannon class tables, Souriau Series III, and dtl38999.pdf Table II (p.3 / p.14).

Bonus real-spec anchor for the blue accent: the connector's interfacial / peripheral seal is fluorosilicone per A-A-59588, "Color Blue" (Glenair) — the blue in the renders maps to an actually-specified part color, not just artistic license.

5 · Internal components spec-cited

Everything behind the coupling ring — pulled from dtl38999.pdf + catalog material tables. Note: standard inserts have no spec'd color (material only); blue, however, is specified twice.

*Standard power/signal inserts are specified by material, not color ("high-grade rigid dielectric / N.A."); rugged I/O modules use black PPS (UL-94). So in realistic mode the insert face renders as a neutral dark dielectric, and the blue fluorosilicone interfacial seal shows as a ring on the pin/plug mating face (try the front-face above in realistic mode).

Why this matters for the palette: blue is a genuine D38999 color twice over — the fluorosilicone seal (A-A-59588) and the EIA-359 rear-release band — and gold is the dominant interior metal. That makes steel/cobalt blue (accent) + contact gold (verified/highlight) the most spec-honest UI accents, not arbitrary choices.
